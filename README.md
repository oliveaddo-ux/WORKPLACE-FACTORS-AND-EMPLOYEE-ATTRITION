## Project Overview

This project analyzes employee and workplace data to understand factors related to employee attrition, recruitment, salary distribution, employee performance, and absenteeism.

The analysis is designed to help management identify workforce patterns and make better decisions relating to employee retention, recruitment, compensation, performance, and attendance.

## Business Questions

The analysis focuses on six major business questions:

1. What are the main reasons employees leave their jobs?
2. Which recruitment channels are the most effective?
3. How does salary vary by gender and department?
4. Which department has the highest average salary?
5. Which age group has the strongest performance results?
6. Which employees have the highest levels of absenteeism and may require management attention?

## Key Areas of Analysis

### Employee Attrition

Termination reasons and employment status are analyzed to understand why employees leave the organization. This can help management identify important retention issues and develop strategies to reduce avoidable employee turnover.

### Recruitment Effectiveness

Recruitment sources such as Indeed, LinkedIn, Google Search, Employee Referral, CareerBuilder, Diversity Job Fair, Website, and other channels are compared to determine how employees recruited through different sources perform and remain with the organization.

### Salary Analysis

Employee salaries are analyzed across gender and departments to identify differences in compensation and determine which departments have the highest average salaries.

The salaries in the original dataset range from **45,046 to 250,000**, with an overall average salary of approximately **69,021**.

### Employee Performance

Performance is grouped into four categories:

**Exceeds, Fully Meets, Needs Improvement, and PIP.**

The analysis compares performance across employee age groups to identify where stronger and weaker performance results are concentrated.

### Absenteeism

Employee absence records are analyzed to identify employees with unusually high attendance issues. This can support further investigation and appropriate management action.
